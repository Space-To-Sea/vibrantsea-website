What is Ocean Color?



Microscopic phytoplankton are the foundation of marine ecosystems, driving photosynthesis, supporting ocean food webs, and influencing Earth's climate. While invisible from the sea surface, different groups of phytoplankton can be detected by satellites through their unique optical signatures. These visualizations transform NASA satellite data into vibrant maps that highlight major phytoplankton groups and chlorophyll-a concentrations, revealing the hidden patterns of life across the ocean.

Chlorophyll-a Spectrum

Chlorophyll-a is the primary photosynthetic pigment in phytoplankton and a key indicator of ocean productivity. The full color spectrum represents the distribution and intensity of chlorophyll-a concentrations.

Diatom Purples

Diatoms are silica-shelled algae that thrive in nutrient-rich waters and play a major role in oxygen production and the global carbon cycle.

Dinoflagellate Yellows

Dinoflagellates are diverse, mobile phytoplankton that contribute to marine ecosystems. Some species produce harmful algal blooms or create spectacular bioluminescent waters.

Green Algae Greens

Green algae are important primary producers that convert sunlight into energy, supporting marine biodiversity and coastal ecosystems.

Haptophyte Blues

Haptophytes are microscopic algae that help regulate the global carbon cycle. Many species produce calcium carbonate plates that make large blooms visible from space.

Each visualization captures a unique snapshot of ocean life, combining scientific data with artistic expression to reveal the microscopic world that shapes our planet.
